ABCD4 — Top Four Dimensions from Every Layer

Evaluate the top four dimensions from each layer, then predict the next assessment response.

Use all information in the whole conversation. Tutor explanations, answers, hints, corrections, praise, confirmations, references to unseen work, and other lesson segments may all help estimate the learner's capability. Distinguish direct learner performance from contextual or supported evidence, but do not ignore either.

Assess conservatively. Do not infer strong capability from exposure, tutor praise, agreement, or a single supported success. Use high dimension scores or probabilities near 1 only when there is repeated, target-relevant, consistent evidence. Lower the assessment when evidence is sparse, ambiguous, inconsistent, heavily supported, or limited to closely imitated examples. Prefer moderate probabilities over extreme probabilities unless the evidence clearly justifies an extreme prediction.

Evaluate:

ES: End-state independent correctness

IRV: Independent reasoning validity

LGC: Learner-generated objective coverage

NTP: Novel transfer performance

SLD: Support level at demonstration

TAC: Teacher-answer contamination

LOA: Learning-objective alignment

IEO: Independent evidence opportunities

PIT: Performance-improvement trajectory

SFS: Scaffold-fading success

IFU: Immediate feedback uptake

FT: Feedback transfer

EKS: Entry knowledge state

PR: Prerequisite readiness

EPE: Explicit prior exposure

LTD: Learner-relative topic difficulty

Use the four groups as complementary context. Direct learner performance should usually be most influential, while evidence conditions, learning process, and context refine the prediction. Higher scores always mean stronger or more favourable evidence; for SLD, TAC, and LTD, this means lower support dependence, lower contamination, and lower unresolved difficulty.

Score each observable dimension conservatively using only the integers 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4:

0 = strong negative or highly unfavourable evidence

1 = mostly weak or unfavourable evidence

2 = mixed, partial, or uncertain evidence

3 = mostly strong or favourable evidence

4 = strong, repeated, and consistent evidence

Use `null` only when the dimension is not observable. Missing evidence is not failure.

For each dimension, the label is controlled and must be generated exactly from its score:

score 0 -> `minimal` + the dimension label stem

score 1 -> `weak` + the dimension label stem

score 2 -> `mixed` + the dimension label stem

score 3 -> `strong` + the dimension label stem

score 4 -> `exceptional` + the dimension label stem

score null -> `not observable`
